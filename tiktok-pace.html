Framework

Project 02

One framework.
Five courses.
One project.

The PACE framework—Plan, Analyze, Construct, and Execute— structured the project from initial data inspection through statistical testing and machine-learning implementation.

Rather than treating each certificate course as a separate exercise, this strategy consolidates the strongest decisions and findings into one continuous analytical narrative.

01

Plan

Define the problem
before modeling it.

TikTok needed a scalable way to distinguish videos containing factual claims from videos expressing opinions. The objective was to build a classification model using engagement, creator, and video-level characteristics.

The intended stakeholders included TikTok leadership, data-science managers, product teams, trust and safety teams, and content-moderation stakeholders.

01

Business objective

Classify videos as claims or opinions and prioritize likely claims for human moderation review.

02

Available data

Claim status, views, likes, shares, comments, downloads, video duration, text length, verification status, and author-ban status.

03

Success metric

Recall was prioritized because missing a claim represented greater business risk than sending an additional video for review.

04

Responsible use

Predictions should support—not replace—human decision-making and require continued monitoring for fairness and bias.

Research Question

Can engagement, creator, and video characteristics accurately distinguish claims from opinions?

02

Analyze

Explore before
you predict.

The dataset was inspected for structure, missing values, distributions, outliers, and relationships between variables. It was consistently formatted, with only a small number of incomplete records.

Engagement variables were highly right-skewed and contained extreme but plausible values. These observations informed visualization choices, statistical testing, feature selection, and model design.

02A

Statistical Investigation

Verification status
warranted testing.

A Welch two-sample t-test evaluated whether verified and unverified accounts had significantly different mean video view counts.

The null hypothesis was rejected. Unverified accounts averaged substantially more views, suggesting verification status could provide useful contextual information during model development.

03

Construct

Translate findings
into models.

The project progressed from visual analysis and hypothesis testing into regression and tree-based classification. Categorical variables were encoded, features were prepared, and model performance was evaluated systematically.

Random Forest and XGBoost were tuned with GridSearchCV and compared using recall, precision, F1-score, accuracy, and performance on unseen data.

01

Visualize

Built scatter plots, bar charts, box plots, and a Tableau dashboard to communicate distributions and relationships.

02

Test

Used hypothesis testing to assess the relationship between account verification and average video views.

03

Regress

Evaluated predictive relationships, model assumptions, coefficients, and potential opportunities for refinement.

04

Classify

Trained Random Forest and XGBoost models to distinguish claims from opinions.

03A

Model Evaluation

Random Forest
emerged ahead.

Both tree-based models achieved excellent validation performance, with recall and precision near 1.0.

Random Forest slightly outperformed XGBoost and produced fewer false negatives, making it the stronger model for a moderation workflow designed to avoid missed claims.

04

Execute

Convert prediction
into decision support.

Video views and likes emerged as the strongest predictors of claim status. Shares, downloads, and comments also contributed meaningful predictive information.

Verification status, author-ban status, video duration, and text length contributed comparatively less than behavioral engagement measures.

01

Prioritize review

Route highly engaging videos likely to contain claims toward human moderation teams.

02

Maintain oversight

Use model outputs as decision support rather than automated final moderation judgments.

03

Monitor over time

Track fairness, drift, and performance as content and platform behavior evolve.

04A

Limitations + Next Steps

Strong results.
Necessary caution.

The supplied dataset may not represent TikTok content across every creator, topic, language, geographic region, or time period. Near-perfect model performance should therefore be validated against broader and more current data.

Future iterations should incorporate richer creator and content metadata, moderation outcomes, posting behavior, and more diverse samples before production implementation.

01

Expand the sample

Include additional topics, creators, languages, regions, and time periods.

02

Add richer features

Incorporate account age, follower count, hashtags, audio, audience characteristics, and moderation outcomes.

03

Validate continuously

Monitor model fairness and drift, then retrain with updated platform data.

04

Investigate variation

Compare performance across creators, subjects, languages, and content categories.

PACE in Practice

The strongest model was not the project’s only output. The process also created a defensible path from business question to responsible implementation.
